## CarnotCodeCarver's Design Strike

**Verdict:** RECAST

**Summary:** no real engine matches the Carnot cycle; a reviewer's job is to say how far short we are. The core move is right: a call needs identity, and channel-as-call is the entropy source behind the backlog. But the candidate overclaims statelessness and underprices the new wire object. The reversible ideal would separate identity, delivery, authorization, lifetime, and history without inventing storage by accident; this design cleanly separates some of them, then quietly re-couples others through replay, two-path delivery, occupancy, and missed-call/product records. Dijkstra: "Simplicity is prerequisite for reliability." The fold-back is: keep call identity on the wire and room keying by call ULID, but recast the design around an explicit minimal call ledger or prove the stateless construction with invariants. Do not let 'not a database row' become theology.

**Fatal flaws:**
- The design says the call is nowhere except the wire, but several required guarantees imply remembered facts: whether an invite was sent, whether an end is lone, whether a charge was paired/reserved, whether a replay has already been consumed, and whether socket/wake/history projections agreed. That is per-call state by another name unless each guarantee is deliberately weakened.
- The authorization function depends on live occupancy, but occupancy is not a stable authorization input. It is a moving observation of an SFU state, not a transaction boundary. The B-C block race is not solved by naming occupancy; it needs a join-time set invariant, a lock/serial gate, or a fail-closed room admission model.
- Section 4 frames 'not a message' as removing permanence, but it may only relocate permanence into APNs logs, socket events, client local state, missed-call UI, analytics, retry queues, and reconciliation paths. The product choice is not 'message or no record'; it is 'which projections exist, with which retention and authority.'
- The invite-as-bearer replay story is underdesigned. Expiry bounds duration of abuse, not multiplicity. If repeated joins, repeated wakes, cross-device replays, or old socket deliveries are harmful, pure stateless signature verification is insufficient. Feynman: "What I cannot create, I do not understand." Here, if you cannot create the failure table, you do not yet understand the object.
- The co-presence check is treated as the authoritative decision that reopens #3170, but the document has not shown the operation that must be authoritative. Is it invite minting, wake sending, token minting, SFU room join, subscription, or ongoing occupancy enforcement? Until the gate is named, the argument is a function wearing a server-shaped hat.
- The v2 one-way-door hazard is larger than stated. Exact-string v1 detection means mixed fleets can silently partition ring behavior; adding socket/wake agreement creates more than a compatibility branch. It needs a version negotiation and rollout invariant, not just island-first sequencing.
- The design dissolves the channel coupling but risks replacing it with participant-set coupling inside signed invites. If the participant set is caller-authored and merely filtered, then group membership, policy freshness, and invite forwarding become correctness-critical. The simpler alternative may be recipient-authored reachability plus per-recipient call admission, not caller-authored gathering membership.
- The lifetime table collapses APNs expiration, ring ceiling, and freshness under invite.expires_at, but leaves room lifetime separate. Good instinct, incomplete thermodynamics: entropy still leaks through retries, clock skew, delayed socket delivery, app resume from history, and older builds. 'Expired' must be defined at every consumer, not just in the island's ideal clock.

**What holds:**
- The central diagnosis holds: channel identity is the wrong room identity. `room_for_call(ulid)` is the high-leverage move, and it dissolves several backlog items that are currently being patched as separate defects.
- The document correctly refuses the false third cell from 12a-RECAST. Silence for a stranger without any enforcement party knowing the relevant edge remains unavailable on this platform.
- Separating wake permission, co-presence permission, and SFU occupancy is a real conceptual improvement. The current `should_wake(kind, body)` predicate is doing thermodynamic nonsense: one valve pretending to be three valves, increasing entropy with every special case.
- Readable signed invite plus fresh island policy is materially better than an irrevocable blind capability for revocation. That distinction is valid and important.
- The call expiry not being the call duration is correct. The design avoids the old mistake where a ring lease can murder a live conversation after answer.
- The v2 compatibility warning is excellent and should survive the recast. Exact equality on invite body is a silent failure machine.
- The document is honest about Section 4 being young. That humility is not decoration; it is load-bearing engineering judgment. Hamming: "The purpose of computing is insight, not numbers." Here, the insight is that transport history has been mistaken for product history.

**If RECAST, what to fold back:**
- Retain the wire call object and call ULID. Make the minimum invariant: every call-bearing wake/socket event carries `call_id`, `kind`, `expires_at`, signed invite digest, and format version; clients reject mismatches with one total fallback path.
- Name the authoritative gate. Preferred fold-back: SFU token mint or room join is the authority for co-presence, not invite mint and not passive occupancy read. Define the invariant as 'no admitted room set may contain a forbidden pair under current policy.'
- Stop asserting 'no row' until the state audit is complete. Produce a table of each guarantee versus whether it needs memory: replay, lone-end suppression, rate reservation, rollout compatibility, missed-call record, multi-device delivery, and socket/wake dedupe. If any row needs memory, price the smallest ledger honestly.
- Consider a tiny ephemeral call ledger with TTL instead of a permanent call row: `call_id`, inviter, participant set hash or list, expires_at, invite_sent flags, wake charge reservation, terminal marker, TTL. If this ledger deletes more machinery than it adds, the anti-row premise should dissolve.
- Recast Section 4 from 'invite stops being a channel message' to 'call invite is no longer authored as channel history; optional history projections are product records.' That prevents transport, audit, and missed-call UI from smuggling permanence back in unnamed.
- Define rollout as a protocol, not a sequence: v1 parse, v2 parse, dual emit or negotiated emit, client minimum version, island compatibility window, and the exact behavior for unknown `k` and mismatched ULIDs.
- Write the failure-mode cage match for co-presence: B blocks C after invite before join, while in room, during reconnect, across devices, across islands, and during selective subscription. The second law says disorder increases unless you pay work at the boundary; name the boundary.
- Re-derive the simpler alternative before building group gathering machinery: recipient-owned interruption policy plus per-call room identity may satisfy the immediate 1:1 and small-gathering needs while postponing caller-authored participant-set invites. Hunt the wasted work first.
