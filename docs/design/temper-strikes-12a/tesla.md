## Tesla, the Arc-Prophet's Design Strike

**Verdict:** RECAST

**Summary:** Three outcomes, three clocks, three arms — and the frequency that shatters the glass is the one both tabs tuned to the same wrong note: that a mandatory CallKit report plus an immediate end is a designed third cell, a lease that is not an inferred end, and one ULID that is unique because it was copied.

**Fatal flaws:**
- **The trilemma did not dissolve; it was recategorized into a cell Apple does not sell.** Headline / 16 v2 §1d treat *silent / momentary / sustained* as a topology, and claim the report-then-end interval is "the on-device window Decision 4 says does not exist." Decision 4 was right about physics and only wrong about grammar. Swift *can* verify before `reportNewIncomingCall` — CryptoKit, App Group, microseconds, all true. Swift *cannot* enact that decision as silence, and it *cannot* enact it as a synchronous retract either. `reportNewIncomingCall` is an async RPC to SpringBoard; the incoming UI is system UI; `reportCall(endedAt:)` before that completion races "unknown UUID" (the ring that cannot be stopped) against a full-screen flash after it (the ring you already had). The "quarter-second buzz" is unmeasured in both documents, and both documents treat that hope as a product Nick can price. Worse: report-and-immediate-end is the iOS 13 abuse pattern Apple wrote the must-report rule to kill. Flaw 9 is not a ratio you tune. It is the 3am revocation of the right to ring anyone. The shared premise neither tab could see: they agreed that *momentary* is a real outcome because they both needed a third cell to keep device-local consent and VoIP in the same design. It is not a decision point. It is a mandatory lie to CallKit, then a prayer. And it does not implement the 2026-09-01 ruling — "default-on for DMs with friends" under (B) is VoIP-for-every-DM-invite, then retract. Non-friends get punch-through-DND plus Recents, not silence. That is a deviation from the ruling wearing the ruling's name. Empty App Group (reinstall, OS wipe, Dart never written through) makes the failure *correlated and fleet-wide*: every friend ring is report-and-end on the same morning, and Apple hears one chord.
- **Ring-lease expiry is a rename of the inference Decision 1 forbids, and the 30/60 beat will leave a late ring with the stop already spent.** Finding 1 / design 12 Decision 1, 1c, 5. You cannot expire a *delivered* push. `_EXPIRATION_SECONDS = 60` is pre-delivery TTL — "do not land late." A lease is a second wake, island-minted, that asks CallKit to tear down UI the first push already created. No client signed it. Decision 5's last bullet still says cancellation remains a signed client message; this is not one. Calling it "a fact about the island's own delivery" is costume. The island must index call ULID → sent-at and emit a stop. That is a one-column call object. Nick's ruling that the island takes the ceiling is not the flaw; the claim that "Decision 1's boundary survives intact" is. The blast at 3am: the document never voids the lease on answer. Island has no call object, so it cannot know the callee picked up at T+8s. At T+30s the "expired push" murders a live conversation unless the *device* treats the wake as "stop ringing, not hang up," which 12a does not pin. Occupancy is declared complementary, not a void-condition. Then the three clocks: invite APNs 60s, lease 30s, freshness 10s, relationship still open after the ceiling was decided. A push that lands at T+45s (legal under the 60s TTL) starts a ring whose stop already fired into a phone that was not ringing. Late invite after spent lease is the inverted failure design 12 exists to prevent, regenerated by unmatched frequencies. Design 12 already wrote the fix — "a VoIP push should expire exactly when the ring stops; the two collapse into one number" — and 12a left the collapse unowned.
- **`ring-starting / not-ring-starting` is the right question; "no new transport" is a lie Decision 2 already disproved.** Finding 3. Alert vs VoIP is not two topics on one wire. Decision 2: a user who declines notifications has a VoIP token and will *never* have an alert token — legitimate, default, not a race. If ends and the lease-stop ride alert, the ceiling Nick just assigned cannot reach the ringing phones that most need it, and a hangup cannot either. If they ride VoIP, Finding 3's stop-becomes-start returns unless the unmeasured `endedAt`-only experiment is YES. The YES/NO tree is honest; the claim that the app tab's request "is satisfied by an island-side change with no new transport" is true only on a branch that Decision 2 says is uninhabitable for a real population. The island-owned 30s ceiling and the end sentinel are the same transport question. Do not harden the predicate as if topic-and-push-type were the whole fork.
- **(C)'s unlinkability is not low-confidence. It is already spent.** "Pushing back on objection (a)" / 16 v2 §1d. A revocation list of stable ids is not a clever construction that *costs* linkability. The id *is* the correlation handle. One-time tokens buy unlinkability of reuse by destroying revocability, as you said — pick two. What you marked LOW is the part that is not a scheme question: the island already stores an attributable invite on a named channel and must hold the device token to wake it. Blindness over a capability cannot un-name a DM. On a self-hosted island the anonymity set is a household, not a crowd; mint-time plus a ring to device D at time T is a name. "Callee publishes this id is dead without revealing whose" reveals the sleeper by the authenticated publisher, then reveals the rest when the stored message arrives. #3745 is correctly kept separate, and that is exactly why (C) cannot sit here as the flaw-9 escape. Flaw 9's actual medicine is *fewer VoIPs that are not calls*, which is (A) or an island-visible proxy, not a panic-minted Chaum token. Leave (C) loaded as the escape and production will grab it at the first Apple warning, long before anyone "who does this for a living" has spoken.
- **Payload ULID ≡ signed ULID "by construction" is an honesty theorem about a copier, not a UUID contract.** Finding 3 UUID / 16 v2 §4. The island never minting and never reusing is vacuously true if it has no call object — it also never *checks*. Clients mint. An attacker who places a live CallKit UUID in their own signed invite is copied faithfully, and `reportNewIncomingCall` on a live id stops call N−1 or collapses two rings into one. Identity as a mutable key, named in 16 v2, "solved" in 12a at the wrong layer. Worse, the two tabs already disagree on authority and neither notices: 12a says the payload UUID *is* the call id; 16 v2 says the payload is a lookup hint and Swift takes the id from the signed body, never the envelope. Must-report makes 16 v2's "never" false: on verify failure there is no trusted signed ULID, so the failure path — the entire momentary cell — *must* report the payload UUID. The only consistent contract is: always report the payload UUID, admit only if signed ULID equals it, never report a second id. Equality is a check, not a construction. And the island half still does not pin that the *end-wake and the lease-wake* carry that same call ULID. Without that, `reportCall(endedAt:)` cannot find the ringing call no matter how faithfully the invite was copied.

A ceiling whose stop can fire before the invite lands is not a ceiling.

Name client-minted reuse of a live ULID as an open hazard under Decision 1's no-call-object constraint, not as something the island "never reuses."
