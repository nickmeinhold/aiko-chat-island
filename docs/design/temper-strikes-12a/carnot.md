## CarnotCodeCarver's Design Strike

**Verdict:** RECAST

**Summary:** no real engine matches the Carnot cycle; a reviewer's job is to say how far short we are. This design is no longer slag: it has correctly dissolved the false binary into silent / momentary / sustained, and it has stopped overclaiming blind capabilities as magic privacy. But it is not yet reversible. The load-bearing moves still depend on unmeasured Apple behavior, a semantic rename around island-issued expiry, and a sender-anonymity story that is deliberately deferred while the design keeps adding attributable signed call artifacts. Dijkstra: "Simplicity is prerequisite for reliability." The fold-back is: freeze the spine as B only for safety, explicitly mark it as non-anonymous, run the one-device CallKit end experiment before choosing end transport, and rewrite Decision 1 as an explicit island-issued ring-timeout control message rather than hiding it behind lease vocabulary.

**Fatal flaws:**
- The ring lease expiry is mostly a rename of an inferred end unless the contract is made sharper. If the island emits a wake at T+30 because no signed end arrived, it is still deciding that the handset should stop ringing without a client-authored stop. Calling it expiry of the island's own push helps only if the client treats it as a separate timeout cause, never as call end, never as caller hangup, never as occupancy evidence, and never as revocation. The current prose gestures at that distinction but still relies on the moral force of Decision 1 surviving. It does not survive by naming alone; the wire semantics must carry the entropy.
- The design accepts B as the spine while leaving the fleet-wide VoIP penalty as a measurement later. That is backwards. The whole B arm depends on report-and-end being rare enough for Apple. If attacker-observable momentary rings can be generated by anyone who can trigger a VoIP invite path, the failure is not merely UX harassment; it is a system-wide privilege-burn attack. The second law applies: you cannot push disorder into Apple's delivery reputation bucket and pretend the local state machine stayed clean.
- The narrowed claim 'only the island can produce silence' is directionally right, but it smuggles in a device capability that remains unverified: that Swift may verify, report, and immediately end inside the PushKit budget without creating unacceptable UI, Recents, analytics, or VoIP-delivery side effects. The document treats the report-then-end interval as a usable design surface before measuring whether iOS treats it as one. Feynman: "What I cannot create, I do not understand." Here: what you have not measured on the handset, you do not own as a premise.
- Decision 4's corrected predicate, ring-starting / not-ring-starting, is the right conceptual frame but not yet a design decision. The document itself admits the top node may moot it: if reportCall(endedAt:) alone satisfies the VoIP must-report rule, end sentinels can remain VoIP. Therefore Decision 4 cannot be amended yet; only the experiment can be queued. A design that records the second branch before resolving the first is doing wasted work.
- The blind-capability arm is still under-struck. 'Unlinkable / revocable / offline-mintable — pick two' is a good slogan, but it understates the coupling: revocation is not just a cryptographic property, it is a distribution, storage, replay, rotation, and multi-device consistency protocol. Once the island stores revocation identifiers, observes APNs device targets, and sees timing, the anonymity claim becomes scheme-specific and probably brittle. LOW CONFIDENCE on unlinkability is not a note; it is a stop sign for keeping C as anything more than a research escape hatch.
- The UUID contract is cleaner than before but still has a dangerous ambiguity between payload hint and signed authority. Island design 12a says payload UUID IS the client-minted call ULID and therefore equals the signed ULID by construction. App design 16 v2 says Swift must verify the signed bytes and take the id from the signed body, never from the payload envelope. Those are not equivalent threat models. Before proof, CallKit needs a UUID from the untrusted payload; after proof, the signed ULID may disagree. The design needs an explicit mismatch procedure: report with payload UUID, verify, if signed ULID differs then immediately end payload UUID as forged and never remap the live call.
- The sender-anonymity contradiction is deferred but not isolated. The ring lease, signed end sentinel, occupancy endpoint, and client-minted call ULID all create attributable call artifacts. Saying #3745 stays separate is procedurally tidy, but architecturally false if these artifacts are merged first and anonymity is solved later against a hardened shape. Hamming: "The purpose of computing is insight, not numbers." The insight here is that safety-first B may be intentionally non-anonymous; write that as a conscious debt, not as an external thread.
- Recents is still priced as a product question, but the technical design has not preserved the option space. If every failed proof must first report a CallKit call using a cached name, the system may already have produced the record before product chooses the ring/record cell. The design needs to establish whether includesCallsInRecents can be set per failed call, updated before end, suppressed after report, or only configured provider-wide. Without that, 'Deanna/Nick decides later' may be an empty switch.
- The three clocks are named but not normalized into an invariant. APNs expiration, ring duration, freshness, push delivery slack, end-buffer retention, and island lease expiry are all facets of one thermodynamic budget: how long a wake remains causally meaningful. The document says they should derive from one another, but the fold is not written. This is exactly where future wasted work will breed.

**What holds:**
- The three-outcome dissolution is the strongest part of the design. Silent / momentary / sustained is the right topology, and it correctly narrows the old false claim from 'no on-device window' to 'no on-device window to silence'. That recast removes a contradiction without pretending the residual is free.
- With B as the spine, opacity and on-device proof can coexist. Verifying against a local consented key set avoids putting caller identity in the APNs payload. The property is correctly bounded to 'no sustained ring before proof', which is the honest strength of the mechanism.
- The withdrawal of C as the recommendation is good engineering hygiene. The document catches its own axis-upgrade from anonymity ruling to ring-gate mechanism, and the revocation asymmetry is real.
- The end-sentinel transport tree is well framed. Measuring whether reportCall(endedAt:) alone satisfies Apple's rule is the correct first discriminator, and it is cheap enough that arguing past it would be design theatre.
- Keeping occupancy separate from signed end sentinel is correct. End messages cover happy path; occupancy covers no-end, cold-start loss, and empty-room answer. Closing the occupancy endpoint would be a classic weaker-instrument substitution.
- The token-kind / partial-unregister blast radius is properly priced. A stale VoIP token after sign-out is not a minor cleanup defect under CallKit; it is a stranger's handset ringing for the previous owner.
- The design is appropriately suspicious of cross-tab agreement. Corroboration is not verification, especially when both tabs share the same Apple-behavior assumptions and the same repo mythology.

**If RECAST, what to fold back:**
- Rewrite the ring lease as an explicit wire-level `ring_timeout` or `ring_lease_expired` control wake with its own reason code and invariants: not signed as caller end, not occupancy, not hangup, not revocation, idempotent, and safe if it races with signed end.
- Block hardening Decision 4 until the one-device experiment is done. The next design revision should contain the measured result, exact iOS/SDK version, observed system behavior, and then one selected branch: VoIP end with endedAt-only, or non-VoIP end with measured locked-handset delivery.
- Resolve the UUID mismatch contract explicitly. Pre-proof CallKit UUID comes from payload as an untrusted handle. Post-proof authority comes from signed ULID. If they differ, end the payload UUID immediately as forged and do not create or migrate state under the signed id.
- Move C to a research appendix, not an escape arm in the main design, until a cryptographer costs the exact blind-token scheme against APNs recipient knowledge, revocation identifiers, timing linkage, replay, multi-device minting, and offline issuance.
- State B's debt plainly: this design makes ringing safer, not sender-anonymous. Add a merge gate that no implementation claims compliance with the 2026-08-25 sender-anonymity ruling merely by adopting B.
- Turn the clocks into one invariant table with formulas, not constants: ring duration, APNs expiration, freshness admission, end-buffer retention, timeout wake delay, and delivery slack. Any value without a derivation remains open.
- Before punting Recents to product, measure the technical degrees of freedom: provider-wide versus per-call behavior, whether failed proof can avoid Recents, whether cached names are written before verification, and whether `reportCall(endedAt:)` produces history entries.
- Adopt the minimal B spine only after the measurements: island may send VoIP for ring-starting invites; Swift verifies local consent and immediately ends failed proof; momentary ring is accepted as a named residual; C remains unchosen unless report-and-end ratio or harassment evidence makes silence mandatory.
