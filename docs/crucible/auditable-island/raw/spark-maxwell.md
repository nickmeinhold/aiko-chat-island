# Maxwell (Claude) — spark

## THE EXPIRING ISLAND — auditability by forced amnesia

Stop trying to prove the operator behaved. You cannot; they own the box, the
literature says every attempt grew an auditor class or a vendor's trust list, and at
N=2 there is nobody to gossip with anyway.

Invert it. **Do not audit the box. Bound how long the box is worth attacking.**

Today the island is a standing accumulation: it holds the roster, the history, the
routing table, the key registry, and it holds them forever, so every question about it
is a question about the operator's character across all of time. That is why it needs
reputation — because it has a past.

So give it no past. Every authority the island holds becomes a **short-lived
delegation minted by the users' own keys**, with a TTL measured in hours. The island
cannot route for you, cannot hold your roster, cannot name you, cannot carry your
history beyond the window, except insofar as live clients keep re-granting it. The
users are already holding the signed originals — the island's copy stops being the
record and becomes a cache with an expiry.

An island that goes rogue does not need to be *detected*. It simply runs out. No
accusation, no proof, no quorum, no auditor, no log. The clients stop re-delegating
because they stopped opening the app, or because they moved, and within the TTL the
rogue island holds nothing but ciphertext it cannot address and a roster that has
decayed to nothing.

And the by-product is the honest version of the thing we hoped a Merkle log would give
us: **an island's standing is not a score it accumulates, it is a lease it must keep
renewing, and it is unforgeable because it is held by the people, not by the island.**
Age-of-history stops being reputation because it stops being the island's property.
You cannot whitewash a pseudonym into having live delegations from strangers.

The federation's hard question — "what vouches for the first island operator?" —
dissolves the same way. Nothing does. Nothing needs to. A brand-new island is exactly
as trustworthy as an old one, which is to say: trusted with an hour, by whoever chose
to give it one.

OH OF COURSE: an island you have to keep re-earning cannot have a reputation, because
it never gets to accumulate anything in the first place.
