**Verdict:** RECAST

**Summary:** The spine you demanded is right, and the panel over-corrected its curses: an abandoned Room is not dead, a process-local book cannot own a host-wide law, and fail-closed against the ghost you just minted is the 3am self-DoS — *today's scientists substituted thread-counts for experiments, and built a structure with no relation to the SFU seat.*

- **Fail-closed is amputation wearing a lock.** D1's restart-storm law FAILS CLOSED on `(room, identity)` while the prior bridge is "unreaped." Reaping waits on the same `disconnect()` the spike left hung at 90s. Unreaped therefore means *until the hung await returns*, which is the definition of never. A hostile teardown does not degrade the fabric. It denies the capability for an unmeasured window, and a process restart — the only way to clear a process-local registry — walks the recovered machine principal back into the SFU ghost that registry never owned. The hang is still one curse wearing three bodies: the await that never returns, the seat that never left, the door you bolted from the inside.

The process is exactly what crashes, is exactly what aiko will SIGKILL without a handler, is exactly what deletes the map and leaves the seat. Host-wide is the wrong boundary anyway: two hosts, one room, one recovered principal, and the mutex is the SFU, not the box. Identity *allocation* was supposed to carry that law, but D4 is an open lifecycle (provision/store/rotate/revoke/recover/transfer) and the spike mints from the SFU *dev key*. Recover *must* reuse the principal or the robot is not itself; that reuse is the crash path; G3/G4/G5 have not touched it. A duplex invariant whose substrate is a drawing is not an invariant. It is a hope the 3am restart will not be the second process.
