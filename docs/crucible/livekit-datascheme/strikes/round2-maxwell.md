## MaxwellMergeSlam's Design Strike

**Verdict:** RECAST

**Summary:** Decision 1's justification is now measured-false, and worse — decision 1 and decision 2 are in direct contradiction, because putting each Room in its own process makes the duplex invariant unenforceable *while technically satisfying its own wording*.

`John McClane: "Welcome to the party, pal."`

**Fatal flaws:**

- **[INTERNAL CONTRADICTION — the illegal move] Decision 1 breaks decision 2, and the wording hides it.** Decision 2 states the invariant as *"one Room per (process, channel_id, island identity)"*. Decision 1 then puts every Room in its **own dedicated process**. Under that topology the stated invariant is **trivially, permanently satisfied** — one process, one Room, always — while the harm it exists to prevent is *entirely unmitigated*: two pipelines on one host, or one pipeline restarted before the old sidecar is reaped (F7's orphan!), each spawn a sidecar carrying the same island identity into the same room, and LiveKit's last-session-wins silently kills one. The invariant is **stated at the wrong granularity**: the real invariant is *one Room per (channel_id, identity) across the whole trust domain*, and `process` was smuggled into the key at exactly the point that makes it unenforceable. In-process, a module-level registry could enforce the real thing. Out-of-process, **nothing can** — there is no longer any shared place to hold the registry. The re-cast did not solve Tesla's duplex collision; it moved it somewhere a guard cannot reach and then wrote an invariant that reads as green.

- **[FALSIFIED PREMISE] Decision 1's stated justification no longer exists, and the design's own falsifier now refutes it.** The re-cast's reasoning is "asyncio teardown leaks, so isolate the process." F1 measured 0.00 threads/cycle and 0.00 fds/cycle in-process across **both** clean (16 cycles) and hostile (8 cycles) teardowns. Critically, arm A of the hostile test *is* the proposed in-process fix: `asyncio.wait_for(room.disconnect(), timeout=T)`, timeout fires, abandon the Room, continue. That is a five-line bounded teardown, and it is **measured leak-free**. The design must either produce a NEW justification for a whole process-supervision subsystem, or accept that a `wait_for` dissolves it. Right now the doc argues from a premise its own spike killed.

- **[WRONG OPTION-FRAME] The axis is BOUNDEDNESS, not process topology.** Both arms hang (F1, F2). Neither leaks threads/fds. The only real discriminator measured is *who can bound the hang and how* — and the design answered a timeout question with a topology. That framing is what let a `wait_for` get overlooked in favour of a supervisor, a kill-escalation ladder, a readiness handshake, a watchdog, and a wire format. Six new mechanisms to avoid one timeout. `Dijkstra: "Simplicity is prerequisite for reliability."`

- **[UNSTATED ASSUMPTION — the one the enthusiasm smuggled in] The design never says WHERE the pipeline process runs, and that single fact flips F7 from a nuisance to a P1.** The blast-radius section names the owner as *"the bridge process (robot host, **or island-local**)"* as though those are interchangeable. They are not. On a robot host, an orphaned sidecar dies at the next reboot and costs nobody anything. Inside the **island gateway container** — a long-lived, single-process-shaped container with no init reaper and no per-child cgroup accounting — an orphan is a permanently-seated SFU participant that nothing will ever clean up, accumulating one per hard restart, on a box that also serves chat. The design cannot price its own blast radius until it names its deployment target, and it currently avoids naming it via an "or".

- **[DEPENDENCY RECORDED AS RESOLVED] Decision 4's machine principal rests on an unexercised path, and the doc downgraded it on reasoning alone.** The Open Variables section correctly identifies that the island is passkey-primary with `/register` force-closed in prod, then downgrades the blocker to "a *provisioning* step" on the strength of a register-passkey-once-then-refresh flow that **has never been run headlessly**. Meanwhile agent identity (#3096, UserKind) is PR#136 — **open, not merged**. So decision 4 depends on unshipped work in a roadmap the design does not control, recorded as settled. That is the same class of error as decision 1: a claim promoted to premise without an execution.

- **[CONSUMER-CLASS ASSUMPTION, stated once and never surfaced] The JPEG wire (F5) is a correctness property masquerading as a cost.** Two lossy codecs in series is fine for inference and wrong for measurement, archival, or anything a robot's vision loop makes a control decision from. The design mentions "inference tolerates loss" once, under backpressure, and then never surfaces it in the URL or parameters. A `webrtc://` that silently double-compresses is a footgun for any consumer who reasonably assumes a DataScheme hands them *the frames*. Loss must be explicit in the contract, not a property of which wire the sidecar happened to pick.

**What holds:**

- **The thesis. `webrtc://` as a first-class aiko transport is RIGHT, and it is now demonstrated, not argued** — F6 has aiko's own unmodified `ImageReadZMQ` reading a LiveKit room off a one-string change. That is the whole point of the candidate and it survived.
- **The frame adapter.** The numpy↔`VideoFrame` impedance match really is ~1 line each way, proven pixel-correct against a known pattern with a positive-controlled comparator (F3).
- **Decision 3 (the `island_url` boundary + capability-scoped token) is untouched by any of this and remains correct** — the token must never be a full user session, and the SFU URL must come from the trusted island. Unproven, but not challenged.
- **Fold-A and Fold-B** (dead-link-≠-idle, fail-closed on a half-open source) are good and are now implemented and demonstrated in the spike.

**If RECAST, what to fold back:**

1. **Restate the duplex invariant at the correct granularity: one Room per `(channel_id, island identity)` across the trust domain — NOT per process — and name its enforcement point.** If the answer is "a dedicated machine principal per bridge instance makes collision impossible by construction," say *that*, and make identity allocation the mechanism. An invariant with no enforcement point is a sentence, not a design.
2. **Re-derive decision 1 from scratch on the measured premises, and state the bounded in-process teardown as the incumbent that must be beaten.** If out-of-process survives, its justification must be something F1/F2/F7 actually support — most plausibly *fault isolation of native crashes* and *not importing livekit into the gateway*, neither of which the doc currently argues. If it does not survive, demote the sidecar to the escape hatch it originally was.
3. **Name the deployment target explicitly (robot host vs island container), and price F7 separately for each.** If island-local is in scope, the orphaned-SFU-seat class needs an owner and a reaper that does not depend on aiko calling `destroy_sources` — because aiko installs no signal handler and will not call it on a hard kill.
4. **Write the kill-escalation ladder into the design as the load-bearing mechanism it is** (SIGTERM → bounded grace → SIGKILL → loud on survival), not as an implementation detail. F2 shows the shape does not work without it.
5. **Re-open decision 4 as a genuine open dependency**, with the headless credential path named as unexercised and #3096/PR#136 named as a blocking prerequisite rather than a provisioning note.
6. **Make lossiness an explicit term of the `webrtc://` contract** — a wire/fidelity parameter with a stated default, so a consumer chooses double-lossy rather than inheriting it.
7. **Stamp the scope:** this strike grades the DESIGN. The implementation is a spike — unmeasured on publish direction, duplex collision, island integration, and long-run RSS.
